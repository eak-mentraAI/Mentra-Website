AI tutors that coach rather than hand over answers are no longer rare. Several free study modes now guide a student step by step, and most students can open one in seconds.

That's good news for a curious student working on their own. A district has a different job. It needs to know that students are learning, that teachers can act on what they see, and that children's data is handled properly.

These five questions focus on that difference. They work for any vendor, including us, and under each one we've said how Mentra answers it. They aren't a complete procurement checklist: the other essentials, such as curriculum fit, accessibility and cost, are listed at the end.

## 1. Can teachers match AI support to the learning task?

Practice, new material and a check of what students can do alone each call for a different amount of help. A tool built for classrooms lets the teacher set that, rather than leaving it to each student.

**Ask the vendor:** Can a teacher set the level of AI help for a specific assignment? Can they switch it off for a check of unaided work? Can a student override that?

**How Mentra answers:** Teachers choose one of four levels for each assignment, and can limit the number of hints per question:

- **No AI Help:** Sprig is off. Use it for a check of what students can do on their own.
- **Hints Only:** Sprig offers small nudges, up to the limit the teacher sets.
- **Guided Support:** Sprig breaks the problem into steps and asks questions along the way.
- **Full AI Assistance:** the most support, such as working through a similar example. Even here, Sprig is designed to coach the student through their own problem rather than solve it for them.

## 2. Can teachers see which progress was the student's own?

A correct answer reached with three hints looks the same on a worksheet as one reached alone. If the tool can't tell those apart, teachers lose the signal they need most.

**Ask the vendor:** Does the teacher see how each answer was reached? Can they check the evidence behind a label?

**How Mentra answers:** The Independence Report labels each answer *on their own*, *with help*, or *repeated what Sprig said*, and every label links to the turn behind it. It also names which students need a look, and why.

These labels are evidence a teacher can interpret, not proof of learning. They show whether help was active and whether an answer echoes Sprig. The teacher still judges what a student understands, which is why the pilot advice below measures unaided work outside the tool.

## 3. Does it say when it doesn't know yet?

Dashboards that always show a number feel reassuring, but a confident read on thin evidence can send a teacher in the wrong direction.

**Ask the vendor:** What does the report show for a student with very little work so far? How does it signal uncertainty?

**How Mentra answers:** Until a student has done enough unaided work across several sessions and topics, the report says "not enough data yet" instead of guessing. You can see this for Jordan above. Recommendations also show their confidence and say when they may be wrong.

## 4. Who decides what happens next?

AI can suggest a next step. It shouldn't take one on its own, especially for a child.

**Ask the vendor:** When the tool recommends something, what happens if the teacher does nothing? Can the teacher change or dismiss it?

**How Mentra answers:** Mentra suggests a next step, and the teacher decides:

- **Approve:** the teacher agrees and acts on it, for example by running a small group. Mentra records the decision and checks back on what changed. It doesn't act on the student by itself.
- **Modify:** the teacher adjusts the suggestion to fit what they know about the student.
- **Dismiss:** the teacher sets it aside.

## 5. How does it protect students and their data?

"Compliant" on a slide isn't enough. Ask about consent, privacy and safety separately, because they answer different worries.

### Consent

**Ask the vendor:** Is consent required for every student? What happens when a student's age is unknown? Can parents withdraw it?

**How Mentra answers:** Every student needs active consent, recorded by a parent or the school, before they use Sprig. If a student's age is unknown, Mentra treats them as under 13. Parents can withdraw consent at any time.

### Privacy and data use

**Ask the vendor:** How long is student data kept? Is it used to train AI models? Which AI providers process it, and under what agreements? How do deletion and export work? How is one district's data kept apart from another's?

**How Mentra answers:** Parents can request deletion, and can export the last 30 days of their child's AI conversations, redacted for privacy. Each district's data is kept separate. Mentra doesn't switch to a backup AI provider unless that provider is approved for student data. Ask us for our retention, model-training and provider terms as part of your review.

### Student safety

**Ask the vendor:** What happens if a student says something concerning? Who is told, and how quickly?

**How Mentra answers:** Every student message gets a safety check before any teaching happens. Filters are tuned for each age band, and replies to a student in distress point them to a trusted adult. Teachers receive safety alerts, marked by how urgent they are.

## Then test it in a pilot you can actually judge

The strongest evidence is your own. Before you start a pilot, agree how you'll know whether it worked:

- **Measure unaided work outside the tool.** Use tasks students do without AI, before and after the pilot.
- **Compare with similar classes.** A comparison group tells you whether a gain came from the tool.
- **Agree the success criteria up front.** Decide what counts as a good result before anyone sees the data.
- **Track teacher time and use.** Note how often teachers act on what they see, and how long it takes.

## Other essentials to check

These matter just as much, and every vendor should answer them clearly:

- **Learning quality:** Is the tutoring accurate, and does it fit how your teachers teach the subject?
- **Curriculum fit:** Which grades and subjects does it cover, and how does it connect to your scope and sequence?
- **Accessibility:** Can every student use it, including students who need larger text, higher contrast, or reading support?
- **Implementation and integrations:** How long does setup take? Does it work with your rostering and learning platforms?
- **Teacher workload:** How much time does it take each week, and what does it give back?
- **Total cost:** What is the full price per student, including any platform fees, training and renewal terms?

For reference, Mentra connects through LTI 1.3 and OneRoster, offers large-text and high-contrast settings, and setup is designed to take two to three weeks with teacher training. We measure teacher time during every pilot rather than promising a number up front.

## A quick checklist

Take this into any vendor conversation:

1. Can teachers set, and switch off, AI support for each assignment?
2. Does the report separate answers reached with help from unaided ones, with evidence a teacher can check?
3. Does it say "not enough data yet" instead of guessing?
4. Do recommendations wait for a teacher's decision?
5. Are consent, privacy and safety each answered clearly, including retention and model training?
6. Will the vendor agree to a pilot judged on unaided work against a comparison group?
